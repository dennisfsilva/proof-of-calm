## What Proof of Calm shows

If your plan has a holding rule, each purchase and each entry of coins added in the Log shows, when
opened, the day it passes that rule, counted from the date you recorded. It reads “Tax-free from”, or
“Long-term rate from” where the rule lowers the rate. Proof of Calm cannot check the rule's other
conditions, so the date alone does not settle how a sale is taxed.

## The tax-year file

With entries in the Log, you can export a tax-year file in Settings, under Currency & data: figures
from your entries for one tax year, for you or an accountant to work from. The file says it is data
from your log, not a tax computation.

## What it does not compute

Proof of Calm works out no taxable gain or loss, applies no tax rate to your figures, tests nothing
against an allowance or a money threshold, and shows no tax due. Portfolio's P/L is not a taxable
gain either: it is not worked out as one.

## When no date appears

Where your plan follows a country's rule with no holding period, no date appears. Where your plan
follows the general profile's rule, no date appears either, but your country may still have a holding
period. No date appears either while your country's rules are not loaded on this device.

A holding period of your own, set in Settings under Hold & sell, dates your purchases in the Log,
while the country cards and the tax-year file keep the country's rule.

## When a rule changes

When Proof of Calm updates the rules for your plan's country, Today shows a dated notice with a
button to review your plan. Your plan keeps its holding rule until you change it.

## How fast rules change

- Portugal, from 1 Jan 2023: crypto gains taxed, with a 365‑day exclusion under conditions (Lei 24-D/2022).
- Germany, from 1 Jan 2024: the private-sales Freigrenze rose from €600 to €1,000 (BGBl. 2024 I Nr. 108).
- Italy, from 1 Jan 2026: the substitute tax rate on crypto rose from 26 % to 33 % (L. 207/2024).
- Belgium, from 1 Jan 2026: a 10 % tax on gains on financial assets, crypto included (law of 6 Apr 2026).
- The UK, 2023 to 2024: the annual exempt amount fell from £12,300 to £6,000; 2024 to 2025: £3,000 (HMRC).
