## Other ways to lose the coins

An exchange can fail with its customers' coins inside. Mt. Gox closed its site on 25 Feb 2014. On
28 Feb 2014 it applied for civil rehabilitation in Tokyo and said about 750,000 bitcoins deposited
by users had disappeared. The US Commodity Futures Trading Commission's complaint of 13 Dec 2022
says FTX and FTX US halted all trading and withdrawals on 10 Nov 2022, and that over $8 billion in
customer deposits were missing.

Trading with borrowed money turns a fall into a forced sale, called a liquidation. CoinGlass's 2025
report counts over $19 billion of liquidations on 10 Oct 2025 alone. That was far above any earlier
single day in its data.

## The order that matters

A common order: first, an emergency fund. The FCA's InvestSmart page says many experts advise a fund
that covers 3 to 6 months of outgoings. Then no borrowed money, and only money that can stay
untouched for years. Last, an amount small enough that losing all of it changes nothing important.

## Keeping coins yourself

A seed phrase is the list of words that restores a wallet, so whoever holds it holds the coins.
People who keep one write it on paper or metal, and never type it into a website or photograph it. A
hardware wallet keeps the keys off the computer and shows each transfer on its own screen. A small
test transfer shows that the address works before the full amount moves. Nobody from support ever
asks for a seed phrase, so a request for one is a scam.

## Six patterns scams repeat

- An exchange platform you had not heard of.
- A stranger who builds trust, then steers the talk to investing.
- Someone posing as support, or as a company you know.
- Any request for your seed phrase.
- A promise of high returns with low risk.
- A look-alike address slipped into your history, so a copied address pays someone else.
