## Other ways to lose it

An exchange can fail with its customers' coins inside. Mt. Gox closed its site on
25 Feb 2014. On 28 Feb 2014 it applied for civil rehabilitation in Tokyo and said about 750,000
bitcoins deposited by users had disappeared. On 10 Nov 2022, FTX and FTX US halted all trading and
withdrawals. The US Commodity Futures Trading Commission's complaint says over $8 billion in
customer deposits were missing.

Trading with borrowed money turns a fall into a forced sale, called a liquidation. CoinGlass's 2025
report counts over $19 billion of liquidations on 10 Oct 2025 alone. That was far above any earlier
single day in its data.

## The order that matters

First, an emergency fund. The FCA's InvestSmart page says many experts
advise a fund that covers 3 to 6 months of outgoings. Then no borrowed money, and only money
that can stay put for years. Last, an amount small enough that losing all of it changes nothing
important.

## Keeping coins yourself

A seed phrase is the list of words that restores a wallet, so whoever holds it holds the coins. It
is written on paper or metal, and never typed into a website or photographed. A hardware wallet
keeps the keys off the computer and shows each transfer on its own screen. A small test transfer
first shows that the address works before the full amount moves. Nobody from support ever asks for
a seed phrase, so a request for one is a scam.

## Six patterns scams repeat

- An exchange platform you had not heard of.
- A stranger who builds trust, then steers the talk to investing.
- Someone posing as support, or as a company you know.
- Any request for your seed phrase.
- A promise of high returns with low risk.
- A look-alike address slipped into your history, so a copied address pays someone else.
