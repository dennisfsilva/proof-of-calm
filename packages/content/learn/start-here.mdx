## What averaging is

Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) means buying the same amount on the same day each month, whatever the
price. When the price is high, that amount buys less of the asset you chose. When the price is low,
it buys more. Over the months, your cost per coin becomes an average of the prices you paid.

## What it does and does not do

Averaging removes timing decisions. The amount and the day are set in advance, so news and price
moves change neither.

Averaging has limits:

- It does not make crypto less risky.
- It does not ensure a profit.
- It does not prevent a loss. A plan can lose money for years, and it can lose all of it.

## Why some people hold

A hold phase starts after the last buy and lasts until the sell window you set opens. Without a sell
window, it has no end date. No new buy comes due during this phase.

Holding is a rule some people follow. Deciding in advance keeps the choice apart from the day's
price. In some countries, the tax on a sale also depends on how long each purchase was held.

A hold phase is a rule, not a forecast. The asset can fall during it and can lose all its value.

## What Proof of Calm is and is not

Proof of Calm is a planner and a log. You set a plan once and record each purchase, wherever the
coins are. It shows where you are in the plan. If your plan has a holding rule, Proof of Calm shows
the day each purchase passes the holding period, counted from the date you recorded. It cannot verify
the rule's other conditions, so those are yours to check.

It is not an exchange, a wallet, a signal service, a tax tool, or an advice service. It shows no
news and no feed. It places no orders. Each purchase happens outside Proof of Calm.

## Where your data is stored

Proof of Calm stores your plan and your entries in the browser or installed app where you recorded
them. Clearing browser data or removing the app can delete them. Export a JSON backup in Settings,
under Currency & data, to keep a copy as of the day you export.

Proof of Calm never holds your keys, your funds, or your exchange logins. It never asks for them.
