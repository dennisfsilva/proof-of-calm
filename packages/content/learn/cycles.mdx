Bitcoin's 4 completed cycles had highs in 2011, 2013, 2017, and 2021. From 2013, the highs came
about 4 years apart, up to the 2025 high so far.

A halving cuts the new bitcoin per block by half, every 210,000 blocks: 28 Nov 2012, 9 Jul 2016,
11 May 2020, and 20 Apr 2024 (mempool.space).

Each line gives a high, its low, the fall, and, from 2013, the months from halving to high. Proof
of Calm computed the months from mempool.space and Bitstamp.

- 2011: high $31.91 on 8 Jun 2011 (Mt. Gox), low $1.92 on 22 Nov 2011 (CoinMarketCap), −94.0 %.
- 2013: high $1,163 on 30 Nov 2013, low $152.40 on 14 Jan 2015 (Bitstamp), −86.9 %, 12.1 months.
- 2017: high $19,666 on 17 Dec 2017, low $3,122 on 15 Dec 2018 (Bitstamp), −84.1 %, 17.3 months.
- 2021: high $69,000 on 10 Nov 2021, low $15,479 on 21 Nov 2022 (Bitstamp), −77.6 %, 18.0 months.
- 2025: high $126,272 on 6 Oct 2025 so far, low $57,735 on 1 Jul 2026 so far (Bitstamp),

## Who plans around it

Some people plan around these dates. The Cycle plan template does: it assumes bitcoin's highs keep
coming about 4 years apart, a forecast that may not come true.

## Why it may not repeat

4 completed cycles are 4 data points, the current one is not final, and the series is measured on
the asset that survived. Exchange-traded funds, miner economics, and interest rates may change what
follows a halving.

## The window is a discipline device

A sell window is the period your plan sets for selling, if it has one. It decides when in advance,
so nobody has to decide now. It is not a forecast, though the Cycle plan's dates rest on one. A
high can come before the window, after it, or not at all. Proof of Calm places no orders: any sale
happens elsewhere.

## Three parts

Of your holding at the window's start, your plan marks thirds in its first and third months and the
rest in its closing month. If the window closes before its third month, the second part moves onto
the closing month. Over 3 months or more, one bad week cannot decide the whole plan.
