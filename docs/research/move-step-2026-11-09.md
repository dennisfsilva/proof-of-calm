# The move between venues: the design check

Research date: **2026-11-09**. Scope: how the Log records coins sent from one venue to another and
what each figure and file does with them. The decision is ADR-0054
(`docs/ADR/0054-move-between-venues.md`); this file holds its evidence. Line references (`file:line`)
are to the code at commit 2c370bf5 (2026-11-09), the base of the branch that adds the kind.

## 1. Method

Three blind designers (lenses: the person, the figures, the scope), a judge who walked ten people along the app's defaults, a judge who audited every reason and recomputed the figures, and a reconciler. 6 agents, about 42 min. The check changed 10 of the planner's 15 answers and showed 9 of its reasons false. The orchestrator's corrections: the move's ADR is **ADR-0054**, since ADR-0053 is the currency ADR of `fix/country-keeps-currency`. `feat/log-move-step` moves to **session 16b**: every merge to main deploys production (RELEASE.md, Release steps 1), so the reader (`feat/move-kind`) ships before the writer. Moves listing self-custody places (ADR-0039 lines 23-25) adds weight to ADR-0039's open question on client-side encryption before M3 sync (lines 76-78). ADR-0054's consequences record this; M3 decides it, not this session.

## 2. The decision table

The planner's first plan answered 13 questions; the check answered them again. Design options A to D
are the designers' and the planner's (B). Where the table says the writer, the Log step that records a
move, it ships one release after the reader (`docs/RELEASE.md`, Versioning).

Amended 2026-11-10 (ADR-0055): Q9's "never from the destination" holds for a move's units received
only. The part of a move's fee that no piece outside its destination covers comes from the
destination's own pieces by the lot rule and leaves the coin. Q10's "the move's uncovered units stay
unmatched" now reads: its uncovered units received stay unplaced, never counted as sold and never
refused; a fee no piece of the coin covers is refused as the move's (`fee-exceeds-held`), never as a
sale. Q10's "Never refuse for a venue reason" stands.

## 3. Reasons shown false

- Q1/Q12 (both judges): adding `from: Venue | null; sent: Units | null` to every entry. In this codebase a nullable key is a required key (entry.ts:35-43). The previous build's strictObject (entry.ts:29) would then set aside every v2 or v3 row the new build writes (rows.ts:184-187), and its BackupFileSchema would refuse those files.
- Q2 (audit): `fee` stays money in the pay currency on moves. defaultFee takes the latest non-null fee of any kind (parts.ts:116, 128-129), so a move's fee would become the next buy's default; portfolio.ts:124 would also add it to the fees total.
- Q5 (the audit's examples G1 and I1, aimed at A's identical claim): 'under global, matching ignores groups, so nothing changes there'. A coin fee takes units from a lot: GB 0.1498 BTC and €6,984 against 0.15 and €7,000; Italy's year-end L2 0.0498 against 0.05.
- Q6 (audit): B does not say that a coin fee lowering P/L conflicts with 'fees shown separately' (DOMAIN.md:370-371).
- Q7 (walk): 'the fee as a listed outflow, not a disposal'. In the US a fee paid in the coin is a disposal (JURISDICTIONS.md:406, us.json:57). The audit adds that B has no column saying where the units were and is silent on the Dutch identity (DOMAIN.md:773).
- Q8 (audit, minor): B cites backup.ts:70-72 for newer-version; the lines are 69-71.
- Q9 (walk's new question): 'take the rest from other venues, as sales do (lots.ts:113)'. That would include the destination, so Ledger's own pieces could be 'moved' to Ledger.
- Q11 (walk): 'the date defaults to today, as the form does'. Coins added open with no date because none can be assumed (form.ts:154-156, 171), and on a back-filled move today's date silently re-matches past sales. The audit adds that B names only `lastVenue`, while form.ts:168 is a second copy.
- Q12 (audit): 'safe behind … a fix that validates imported entries' is unexplained, since backups are already validated entry by entry (schema/backup.ts:18). '51 places in 21 files': core alone has 19 files, and core plus the app has 32.

## 4. The worked example (the audit's P1, Portugal)

Portugal matches sales FIFO per platform. Bought: L1, 0.10 BTC at Bitvavo for €2,000 on 2025-01-10;
L2, 0.05 BTC at Kraken for €3,000 on 2025-02-01. On 2025-06-01 the person sends 0.05 BTC from Kraken
to Ledger and 0.0498 BTC arrive: the network took 0.0002 BTC.

- The move takes from its source, Kraken, so from L2, the fee first: 0.0002 BTC leave L2 at a cost
  share of €3,000 × 0.0002 ÷ 0.05 = €12.00. The 0.0498 BTC left stay in L2 at a place at Ledger
  since 2025-06-01, at a cost of €2,988.00.
- Invested stays €5,000 and the average cost does not change; units drop to 0.1498 BTC. At €80,000
  the value is €11,984 and P/L €6,984 (0.15 BTC and €7,000 without the fee).
- The 2025 per-lot file lists L1's year-end piece of 0.10 at Bitvavo; L2's fee piece of 0.0002 dated
  2025-06-01 with a cost basis of €12.00, no proceeds and no `past_holding_rule`; and L2's year-end
  piece of 0.0498 at €2,988.00, `held_at` Ledger, `moved_on` 2025-06-01. No sale took units from
  another venue: `crossVenue` 0. The fixture is `packages/core/test/tax-year/pt-2025-move.csv`.
- Under global matching the fee takes units from a lot all the same: the UK's 0.1498 BTC and €6,984
  against 0.15 and €7,000 (the audit's G1), Italy's year-end L2 0.0498 against 0.05 (I1).
