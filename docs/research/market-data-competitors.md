# Proof of Calm research: market data and competitors

Prepared 2026-10-16 for the Proof of Calm planning session. All sources were accessed on 2026-10-16 unless stated otherwise. Confidence is given per claim as high (primary source read directly, or verified by a live call), medium (primary source paraphrased by a fetch tool, or a consistent secondary source) or low (single secondary source, or inference). Nothing here is legal advice; the licensing verdicts are a developer's reading of the published terms.

Sections: 0 executive summary; 1 CoinGecko; 2 alternative data sources; 3 competitors and positioning; 6 adjacent facts; 7 contradictions with the product assumptions; appendix A verbatim CoinGecko clauses.

## 0. Executive summary

1. **CoinGecko** (API Terms, latest version 5 Sept 2025, read verbatim; live API checks 2026-10-16). (a) Caching prices server-side for minutes to hours and serving them to users: permitted on every plan, because section 6.1 says "if you must cache or store Data:- You should refresh the cache at least every 24 hours; Strong encryption and other security measures should be applied to stored Data", and section 4.1.6 says "You are entitled to charge for your services and products that incorporate or integrates our CoinGecko API." Demo carries no commercial licence (the pricing table gives it only "Attribution required"; the FAQ calls it "a free plan to try out the CoinGecko API. It's good for testing and exploration"), so it fits the free pre-revenue phase only; move to Basic ($35/month) before the paid tier. (b) Storing daily closes since inception and shipping them in the repository: not permitted on any plan; impossible on Demo and Basic anyway (Demo: "Historical data via the Demo API is restricted to the past 365 days", verified live as HTTP 401 error 10012 "Public API users are limited to querying historical data within the past 365 days"; Basic: "restricted to the past 2 years"), and on Analyst and above it breaks 6.2 ("you are not allowed to duplicate, reproduce, copy, store, derive from or translate any Data ... or any information expressed by the Data"), the 24-hour cache rule and the delete-on-termination duty (6.1.4, 10.4.3); the only "Commercial redistribution license" is Enterprise. Derived weekly or monthly series do not cure it. (c) Letting users export their own log with prices: acceptable in practice on every plan with attribution in the file and user-entered fill prices as the primary price field; not a bulk price download. Attribution is mandatory on every plan: "Powered by CoinGecko" (or the brand guide's "Price data by CoinGecko" with a link) in a font "no smaller than font size 10", next to the data. EUR, USD, GBP and CHF are all supported vs_currencies (verified live), but they are converted quotes, not traded EUR prices.

2. **Data sources.** Live prices: CoinGecko `/simple/price` primary (one call for all coins and currencies every 5 minutes is 8,640 calls a month, inside Demo's 10,000), Kraken public Ticker as fallback (free, EUR and USD pairs for all four coins), Coinbase Exchange public ticker as second fallback; ECB reference rates to derive missing GBP/CHF quotes. Bundled history: never CoinGecko. Use Kraken's official bulk OHLCVT download (EUR pairs; BTC from 2013-09, ETH from 2015-08, LINK from 2019-09, SOL from 2021-06) as the base, Bitstamp OHLC as the second leg (BTC/USD from 2011-08-18; the only exchange with a published commercial-redistribution route, via a signed Data License Agreement), Coinbase for cross-checks, and ECB rates (free with attribution) for USD/GBP/CHF conversion. Publish the result as a "Proof of Calm reference close" dataset of our own (for example the median of three exchange closes), daily or weekly and monthly only, never raw exchange files, in a separate package with a DATA-LICENSE.md naming sources. Excluded: CoinCap ("not suitable for commercial use"), CoinDesk Data (free tier retired 2026-05-21; internal use only), Binance (CC BY-NC-SA, "Commercial licensing is strictly excluded", EU service impaired), Coin Metrics Community (CC BY-NC), Blockchain.com and Yahoo (non-commercial), CMC and CoinPaprika (no storage or redistribution below Enterprise, though their paid tiers are the cheapest licensed live feeds if ever wanted).

3. **Competitors.** The three most relevant: Blockpit Plus (€47.90, €59 from 1 Nov 2026) already ships a Germany-only holding-period tracker and real-time sale simulator, backward-looking, no plan; 21bitcoin already ships savings plan plus Haltefrist-Tracker, BTC only, DE/AT, custodial; costavg.com is the simulator benchmark to clear (Kraken data, ECB FX, fee presets, lump-sum comparison, EUR-native, ad-funded). Strike "Stacks" (2026-10-07) shows exchanges productising named plans. One-line differentiation: the only custody-free planner that turns a DCA plan into a dated, per-lot, country-aware sell window across venues, with no news, no alerts and no affiliate incentive. Table stakes: CSV import and export, accurate closes with an "as of" timestamp, EUR/USD/GBP/CHF, per-coin and portfolio charts, lump-sum comparison, fee presets, mobile-first, dark mode, a public pricing page and a privacy page. Trust signals that work: open source or at least a public data pipeline, "no API keys, no funds" stated plainly, a security page, dated country-rule pages with a changelog, "no ads, no selling data".

6. **Adjacent facts.** Euro-area crypto ownership about 9 to 10% (ECB SPACE 2024: 9%, up from 4% in 2022; ECB CES Nov 2024: 9.7% of households, 54% holding under €1,000); Germany 6 to 8% (Bundesbank, Bitkom); Portugal 12% in SPACE (secondary reading) but 1.5% in the national face-to-face literacy survey. The DCA habit is unmeasured officially; the only direct measure is PwC Strategy& 2026 (50% of active crypto investors in five markets use savings plans; 37% of German crypto investors). No broker publishes a crypto share of savings plans.

7. **Contradictions with the product assumptions** (section 7): the history-depth targets (BTC 2010, LINK 2017, SOL 2020) are not achievable from bundle-able data; the German one-year rule is under a live BMF draft (2026-09-30) that would abolish it for purchases after 2026-12-31, not adopted on 2026-10-14 but not dead; "free core forever" carries $35 to $129 a month of data cost once the paid tier exists; "plan plus holding awareness" is partially occupied by 21bitcoin and Blockpit; CoinGecko's non-USD quotes are conversions; CSV import behind a paywall cuts against table stakes; and the name must change.

### 1.2 Historical-data depth: the 365-day limit, verified live

- Demo docs, https://docs.coingecko.com/demo/reference/coins-id-market-chart, .../coins-id-market-chart-range, .../coins-id-ohlc and .../coins-id-history, accessed 2026-10-16, each carry the same warning callout, verbatim: **"Historical data via the Demo API is restricted to the past 365 days."** and **"Subscribe to Analyst plan & above for the full range."** Confidence high.
- Pro docs for the same endpoints (https://docs.coingecko.com/reference/coins-id-market-chart etc.), accessed 2026-10-16, verbatim: **"Historical data on the Basic plan is restricted to the past 2 years."** Analyst and above provide the full range. Confidence high.
- When it changed: CoinGecko Learn, "How to Import Live Crypto Prices into Excel Spreadsheets", https://www.coingecko.com/learn/import-crypto-prices-excel, "Updated May 04 2026", verbatim: **"As of February 2024, access to historical data via the Demo API public plan is restricted to the past 365 days."** Confidence high. Corroborated by developer reports from March and April 2024 (Electron-Cash issue #2825, 2024-03-30, https://github.com/Electron-Cash/Electron-Cash/issues/2825; Portfolio Performance issue #3896, April 2024, https://github.com/portfolio-performance/portfolio/issues/3896), confidence medium. The docs changelog (https://docs.coingecko.com/changelog) has no entry for the change.
- Live verification on 2026-10-16 at 16:09 UTC against `https://api.coingecko.com/api/v3` without a key: `coins/bitcoin/market_chart/range?vs_currency=eur&from=1367107200&to=1372291200`, `coins/bitcoin/market_chart?vs_currency=eur&days=max`, `coins/bitcoin/ohlc?vs_currency=eur&days=max` and `coins/bitcoin/history?date=30-12-2017` all returned **HTTP 401** with `error_code 10012` and the message, verbatim: **"Your request exceeds the allowed time range. Public API users are limited to querying historical data within the past 365 days. Upgrade to a paid plan to enjoy full historical data access: https://www.coingecko.com/en/api/pricing."** Confidence high. Note the status is 401, not 400, which misleads clients into treating it as an authentication failure.

So: `market_chart`, `market_chart/range`, `ohlc` and `history` all share one plan-based time-range gate (365 days on Demo and keyless, 2 years on Basic, full from 2013 on Analyst and above). Proof of Calm's "BTC from 2010, ETH from 2015" history cannot come from CoinGecko on Demo or Basic at all, and CoinGecko's own daily data only starts in 2013 anyway.

### 1.6 Is commercial use permitted on the Demo plan?

Not expressly prohibited, but not licensed either. Evidence:

- API Terms section 4.1.1, verbatim: **"The scope of use that is granted by our licence to you hereunder this API Terms will be dependent on the usage plan that you select, pursuant to each usage plan's corresponding description as set forth at https://www.coingecko.com/en/api/pricing ("CoinGecko Crypto Data API Plans"). In the event that you select one of our paid usage plans, the licence granted to you will include the permitted use as indicated in the description thereunder your selected paid plan, subject to your timely payment of the applicable fees thereto."**
- The pricing table marks Basic, Analyst, Lite and Pro "Commercial license" and Demo only "Attribution required"; the FAQ answer on charging for products lists only the paid plans; Demo is described as "to try out the CoinGecko API. It's good for testing and exploration".
- Section 4.1.6, which permits charging ("You are entitled to charge for your services and products that incorporate or integrates our CoinGecko API."), is not plan-specific, which is the only textual hook for using Demo commercially.

Reading: a free, pre-revenue Proof of Calm can run on Demo with attribution at low-to-medium risk (CoinGecko visibly tolerates this, but section 3.2 lets them change the API or terms "without any notice to you" and section 10.3 lets them terminate "without notice or reasons"). The moment a paid tier exists, the product is commercial and should be on Basic ($35/month) at least. Confidence medium-high.

### 1.7 Terms clauses on caching, storing and redistributing data

API Terms of Service, https://www.coingecko.com/en/api_terms, Gecko Labs Pte. Ltd., "Latest Version: 5 Sept 2025", read from the raw HTML on 2026-10-16; governing law Singapore. Section numbers are inferred from order and confirmed by the document's internal cross-references ("Clause 4.1.1", "clauses 6.1.3, 6.1.4, 6.2", "Section 10"). Confidence high. The full text of the decisive clauses is in appendix A; the decisive sentences:

- Definition: "Data" means "(i) any data and content uploaded, posted, transmitted or otherwise made available by CoinGecko on its website www.coingecko.com or other associated sites/platforms/channels (including but not limited to ... market data on cryptocurrency ...)".
- Grant (3.1): "a limited, non-exclusive, non-assignable, non-transferable and irrevocable license to use the CoinGecko API to develop, test, and support any software application, mobile application, website, platform, service or product, as well as to integrate or incorporate the CoinGecko API with your Application."
- 4.1.6: **"You are entitled to charge for your services and products that incorporate or integrates our CoinGecko API. However, you are not permitted to sell, rent, lease, sub-license, re-distribute or syndicate access to the CoinGecko API or part thereof (unless pursuant to the terms of an Executed Agreement that you enter into with CoinGecko)."**
- 6.1 (Data Caching and Storage): **"We do not encourage caching or storage of Data. However, if you must cache or store Data:- 6.1.1 You should refresh the cache at least every 24 hours; 6.1.2 Strong encryption and other security measures should be applied to stored Data;"** ... **6.1.4 "In the event that CoinGecko terminates your access to or use of the CoinGecko API (regardless of reasons), you agree to promptly and permanently delete all Data and any other information that you have stored pursuant to your use of or access to the CoinGecko API, without keeping any copy thereof unless required by applicable law."**
- 6.2: **"Except as expressly permitted hereunder this API Terms, you are not allowed to duplicate, reproduce, copy, store, derive from or translate any Data, API Documentation, or any information expressed by the Data (including but not limited to hashed or transferred data)."**
- 7 (User Agreement for your Products): if the product is offered to others you must have a user agreement and privacy policy that "(a) identify the CoinGecko API as being the property of CoinGecko; (b) ensure that your users/customers abide by terms and conditions in your user agreement that are no less protective of CoinGecko than the provisions of this API Terms; ... (f) stipulate that you are solely responsible for all disclosures, disclaimers, and warnings to your users/customers regarding the nature, accuracy, and limitations of the data provided through your Products".
- 10.4 (on termination): "10.4.2 You undertake to promptly destroy the API Documentation, Data and any other information procured from CoinGecko ... 10.4.3 ... you undertake to promptly and permanently delete all Data and other information procured from or relating to CoinGecko ... that you have stored in relation to your access to or use of the CoinGecko API. You agree to certify in writing the aforesaid destruction of stored Data and information should CoinGecko request such certification from you." Clauses 6.1.3, 6.1.4 and 6.2 survive termination (10.5).
- 4.1.7(c): no use of Data "in any advertisements or for targeting advertisements".

Website Terms and Conditions (incorporated by reference), https://www.coingecko.com/en/terms, "Latest Version: 12 August 2025", accessed 2026-10-16, confidence medium (fetched via summariser): site content is licensed "solely for your Personal Use ... only and not for any commercial purpose" (4(B)(1)); "shall not howsoever mine data from, scrape, interfere or attempt to interfere with any part of our Site" (9.1). So the CSV/XLS download button on coingecko.com coin pages is personal use only and is not a route to a bundled commercial dataset.

### 1.8 Verdicts

(a) Cache prices server-side for minutes to hours and serve them to our users

| Plan | Verdict | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Demo | Permitted mechanically: 6.1 allows caching if refreshed at least every 24 hours and stored with "strong encryption"; serving cached prices inside Proof of Calm's own UI is "integrate or incorporate the CoinGecko API with your Application", not redistributing "access to the CoinGecko API". Caveat: Demo carries no commercial licence (1.6). Attribution required. | high on caching, medium on Demo scope |
| Basic, Analyst, Lite, Pro | Permitted, commercial licence included, charging allowed by 4.1.6. Keep the cache refresh under 24 hours (minutes is fine), encrypt at rest, delete on termination, attribute. | high |

Budget check: one `/simple/price` call for all coins and all four currencies every 5 minutes is 8,640 calls/month, inside Demo's 10,000; every minute is 43,200 and needs Basic (100,000).

(b) Store daily closes for a handful of coins since inception and ship them as static files in the repository (open or closed), serving them in charts and the simulator

| Plan | Verdict | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Demo / keyless | Not permitted and not possible: 365-day gate (1.2), no commercial licence, 6.2 prohibits copying and storing beyond the 24-hour cache carve-out. | high |
| Basic | Not permitted: 2-year gate plus the same 6.1/6.2/10.4 problems. | high |
| Analyst, Lite, Pro | Retrievable (daily from 2013, not 2010), but shipping it as a static file in a repository is storage and reproduction outside the 6.1 cache carve-out ("refresh the cache at least every 24 hours", "strong encryption"), a copy that cannot be "promptly and permanently delete[d]" (6.1.4, 10.4.3) once it is in git history or a public release, and, in a public repository, plainly "re-distribute or syndicate" (4.1.6). The pricing page reserves the "Commercial redistribution license" for Enterprise. Derived weekly or monthly series do not cure this: 6.2 covers "derive from ... any information expressed by the Data". A private, encrypted, nightly-refreshed server-side copy of the daily series that is deleted on termination is defensible as a 6.1 cache; a committed file is not. | high for the public-repo case; medium for private storage |

Consequence: CoinGecko cannot be the source of the bundled history. Section 2 covers sources whose licences allow it.

(c) Let users export their own log with prices

| Plan | Verdict | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| All plans | Acceptable in practice. The log (dates, coins, amounts, fees) is the user's own data and the fill price should be the user's own number (typed or imported). A CoinGecko reference price attached to a few dozen personal rows is incidental use of the licensed Application, not "syndicat[ing] access to the CoinGecko API". The terms do not address end-user export; a literal reading of 6.2 covers any copy, so keep the export per-user, small, with an attribution line in the file header, and never offer a bulk "download price history" button. On Demo the commercial caveat of 1.6 applies. | medium-high |

### 1.9 Practical consequences for Proof of Calm

1. Live prices: CoinGecko `/simple/price` on Demo for the free pre-revenue phase, with a 5-minute server cache and "Price data by CoinGecko" under every price; move to Basic ($35/month, 10-second freshness, 100k credits) before the paid tier launches.
2. Recent charts (last 365 days on Demo, 2 years on Basic) can come from `market_chart` with `interval=daily`, cached server-side, refreshed daily.
3. Long history and the simulator must use a different, redistributable source (section 2). Do not mix: attribute each series to its real source (brand guide: "Do not attribute CoinGecko for data from another provider").
4. Terms section 7 obliges Proof of Calm to have a user agreement and privacy policy that name CoinGecko and carry the data disclaimers, even for the no-account mode.
5. Expect the 10012 error as HTTP 401; handle it as a range error, not as a key failure.

### 2.4 Recommendation

Live prices (EUR/USD/GBP/CHF)

- Primary: CoinGecko `/simple/price`, which section 1.8 clears for server-side caching on every plan (Demo for the free phase, Basic once the paid tier exists); it quotes all four currencies natively.
- Fallback: Kraken public Ticker (free, no key, EUR and USD pairs for all four coins, GBP for BTC and ETH, few CHF pairs), one multi-pair call per minute cached server-side; derive missing GBP/CHF quotes from EUR with the ECB daily rate and label them "EUR x ECB reference rate". Second fallback: Coinbase Exchange public ticker (EUR/USD/GBP). Neither exchange grants a written display right; aggregators' paid tiers do (CoinPaprika Starter $99/mo with native EUR/GBP/CHF, CMC Startup $79/mo) when revenue justifies it. CoinCap is not a fallback (500 free credits per month and a ToS that says the service is "not suitable for commercial use").

Bundled history

- Base: Kraken bulk OHLCVT (EUR pairs, free, official download, quarterly refresh). Second leg: Bitstamp OHLC, the only source with a published commercial-redistribution route; request the Data License Agreement at partners@bitstamp.net and ask the price for a four-coin daily-close use. Third leg: Coinbase for cross-checks. Email Kraken's API or institutions team asking for written confirmation that publishing derived daily closes with attribution is acceptable, and keep the reply.
- FX: ECB reference rates from the static files for USD/GBP/CHF conversions of EUR closes, with the ECB attribution and the modification statement.

### 2.5 Licensing position for shipping closes in the repository

- Permitted in writing: ECB rates (attribution); Bitstamp (once the DLA is signed).
- Forbidden: CoinGecko, CMC, CoinPaprika, CryptoCompare (internal or integrated use only; CoinPaprika Enterprise excepted); Binance Vision and Coin Metrics (NonCommercial); Blockchain.com and Yahoo (personal or non-commercial).
- Grey, no written licence: Kraken and Coinbase public endpoints.
- Mitigations that materially lower risk: (1) publish a "Proof of Calm reference close", for example the median of the Kraken, Bitstamp and Coinbase daily closes, as a derived dataset of our own with a DATA-LICENSE.md naming sources and dates; never commit raw exchange files; (2) store only what the product needs (daily close, or weekly and monthly closes for the simulator) rather than full OHLCV, which keeps any extraction insubstantial under the EU database right and clearly derived; (3) convert with ECB rates and say so; (4) keep the dataset in a separate package or repository so the app code's licence is unaffected; (5) be explicit in the UI about start dates and that closes are reference values, not tradable prices, mirroring the ECB's own wording.

### 3.4 Does anyone already do "plan plus tax-holding awareness"?

Yes, in pieces, and one product does both:

- 21bitcoin combines a savings plan with a Haltefrist-Tracker. Limits: BTC-only, DE/AT, custodial by default, sees only its own trades, no multi-venue log, no PT rule.
- Blockpit Plus (€47.90, rising to €59/yr from 1 Nov 2026) has a Holding Period Tracker ("Tax-Free Holdings DE"), a real-time sale simulator and tax-loss harvesting: the holding half of Proof of Calm's loop, backward-looking, Germany-only, no forward plan.
- CoinTracking's "Tax-free coins – Short & Long" report and AI Tax Saver (DE only) cover the same half.
- Koinly tags long/short lots by country setting but "does not apply long-term holding discounts".
- bitcoindollarcostaverage.com is the only DCA calculator with a holding-period dimension (US short/long), and it looks abandoned.
- Strike Stacks shows exchanges productising "plans" (buckets with schedules), without tax.
- Not found anywhere: a custody-free, multi-coin, multi-venue, country-aware (DE/PT/AT/FR) forward planner with a sell window and per-lot holding countdown, usable without an account. The combination is unoccupied; every component exists somewhere.

Regulatory caveat that changes the pitch (see also section 7): a German BMF draft of 2026-09-30 would remove the one-year rule for crypto "acquired after 31 December 2026" (flat 25% under §20 EStG); the cabinet date of 2026-10-14 passed without a decision and the draft is in inter-ministerial coordination (CryptoTicker 2026-10-15; Blockpit guide 2026-10-09: "Beschlossen ist das noch nicht"). If enacted, per-lot date tracking matters more, not less (grandfathered pre-2027 lots against new lots), but "tax-free after one year" stops being a universal German hook. Austria already has no holding period (27.5% KESt since 2022-03-01). Portugal has 365 days with a 28% short-term rate, and sources disagree on whether crypto-to-crypto swaps reset the clock. Country rules must be dated, versioned and shown with a "Stand" line as Blockpit does.

### 3.5 Positioning analysis

Genuinely differentiated

1. Forward plan plus per-lot holding countdown by country, across venues, without custody. Trackers only look back; calculators ignore tax; 21bitcoin does both only inside its own BTC funnel.
2. The "no news, no sentiment, no alerts, ever" contract. Only Rotki, Kubera and Pocket are comparably calm, and none is a planner. Be honest internally: noise sells (CoinStats and Delta, at 1M+ downloads each, are the noisiest products in the set), so calm is a niche with a willingness-to-pay signal (Kubera at $250/yr) rather than a mass market.
3. Usable without an account and no API keys. Every tracker demands API keys or addresses; only Pocket (account-less buying) and Rotki (local) make a similar promise.
4. Templates not recommendations, no affiliates. Every free calculator and media tool monetises through exchange affiliate links; exchanges funnel into their own product.
5. Honesty as a feature is credible only if the simulator is at least as rigorous as costavg.com (Kraken data, ECB FX, fee presets, lump-sum comparison, blunt disclaimer). costavg is the benchmark to clear, not the typical "DCA calculator".

Table stakes Proof of Calm must match

- CSV import of a purchase log (every tracker; 21bitcoin and many brokers export CSV only) and CSV/PDF export of log and plan.
- Accurate daily closes plus a live price with a visible "as of" timestamp; EUR/USD/GBP/CHF.
- Portfolio and per-coin charts, lump-sum vs DCA comparison, fee modelling with venue presets (costavg, MissCrypto).
- Mobile-first responsive or PWA; competitors are all native apps. Dark mode is standard across the set (not individually verified).
- A clear public pricing page (every competitor has one) and a privacy/GDPR page.
- Later: import templates for the venues EU users actually use (Bitvavo, Kraken, Bitpanda, Coinbase, Trade Republic, Relai, 21bitcoin, Pocket).

Trust signals the best products use

- Open source and local-first (Rotki: AGPL-3.0, "never passes through rotki servers", SQLCipher, 4k stars).
- Plain custody statements: "we don't hold or manage your funds" (Delta), "does not require any private keys" (Koinly), segregated custody plus insurance (21bitcoin).
- Specific security pages (Koinly: aes-256-gcm for keys, pentests, bcrypt), third-party audits (CoinStats: Hexens), SOC 2 (CoinTracker).
- Transparent fee and pricing policies with worked examples (Relai pricing policy; 21bitcoin "ab Tag 8: 0 %"; Finanzfluss total cost per €500).
- Dated, versioned tax content with update logs (Blockpit: "Steuerstand: 2026", update log 2026-10-08) and published product changelogs.
- "No ads, no selling data" (Kubera); regulatory badges where applicable (MiCA CASP, FMA, AMF).

For Proof of Calm the analogues are: a public repository or at least a public data pipeline and a /data-sources page, a /security page stating "no keys, no funds, no API keys, no exchange connections", EU hosting, dated country-rule pages with a changelog, and a pricing page that says "free core forever" in writing.

Main threats

1. An exchange ships the loop. 21bitcoin already has plan plus Haltefrist for BTC in DE/AT; Bitpanda, Bitvavo or Trade Republic adding a "tax-free from <date>" badge is one sprint because they hold the lots; Strike Stacks shows exchanges moving into named plans. Defence: multi-venue neutrality, a cross-exchange log, self-custody friendliness, honesty, and no incentive to sell product.
2. A tax tool adds a forward planner. Blockpit already has the holding tracker and sale simulator and is expanding Plus; CoinTracking has the AI Tax Saver. Defence: pre-purchase use (they only see the past), calm UX for savers rather than traders, free core, no API keys.
3. SEO incumbents: costavg, MissCrypto, Finanzfluss and the US calculators own "DCA calculator / Sparplanrechner" with affiliate budgets. Defence: EUR plus holding-period plus plan angles, and content that is dated and honest.
4. Regulation: the German draft could remove the one-year rule for post-2026 purchases in the largest EU market; design the rules engine for "no holding period" (Austria today) and for grandfathered lots.
5. Native-app expectations and data-licensing cost (sections 1 and 2).

One-line differentiation: the only custody-free planner that turns a DCA plan into a dated, per-lot, country-aware sell window across venues, with no news, no alerts and no affiliate incentive.

Gaps not closed (search budget): Trade Republic's primary crypto fee page, Coinbase's recurring-buy fee page, Pocket's fee FAQ text (429), dcaBTC's calculator UI; Rotki's "tax-free after period" setting and Bitpanda's tax-report features are from memory and marked medium.

### 6.5 Suggested use in positioning

Roughly one in ten euro-area adults owns crypto (ECB SPACE 2024: 9%, up from 4% in 2022; CES Nov 2024: 9.7% of households), overwhelmingly as an investment (82 to 92% in DE and NL) and in small amounts (54% under €1,000). Germany is the laggard (6 to 8%), Portugal an above-average adopter on ECB measures. Regular-plan buying is already the dominant retail method among active crypto investors (PwC Strategy& 2026: 50% use savings plans; 37% of German crypto investors, the same as for stocks), and ETF savings plans, the behavioural template Proof of Calm borrows, run at about 5.8m executions a month at German banks excluding neobrokers and about 15m across continental Europe. Nobody publishes how many of those plans are crypto; that gap is itself a positioning point. The behavioural literature (BIS 2023; Kogan et al. 2024) documents that retail enters after price rises and trades momentum in crypto, the exact failure mode a discipline-first planner addresses.

## 7. What contradicts or strains the product assumptions

1. **History depth.** "BTC from 2010, ETH from 2015, LINK from 2017, SOL from 2020" is not achievable from any source that may be bundled (section 2.3). Realistic bundled start dates: BTC 2011-08 in USD (Bitstamp) or 2013-09 in EUR (Kraken), ETH 2015-08, LINK 2019-09, SOL 2021-06. CoinGecko's own daily series start in 2013 and may not be bundled on any plan (section 1.8 b). Pre-2011 BTC and 2017 to 2019 LINK exist only in aggregates or on Binance-type venues whose licences forbid redistribution. The simulator should say "from the first liquid EUR market" and show the date, which also fits "honesty is a feature".

2. **The German one-year rule is a live political variable, not a constant.** The BMF Referentenentwurf of 2026-09-30 would move crypto gains to §20 EStG at a flat 25% plus Soli (26.375%) and remove the holding period for assets acquired after 2026-12-31, with grandfathering of earlier lots (Ecovis, Ledger, CPM Steuerberater, kleinstb.de, all October 2026). It was not adopted: the cabinet session of 2026-10-14 passed without it (CryptoTicker "Kabinettstermin ohne Beschluss", kryptorevolution.de "vorerst vertagt"), no new cabinet date exists, and the Bundestag rejected a separate Greens' bill to abolish the holding period on 2026-10-08. Consequences for the plan model: the holding rule must be versioned by acquisition date (per-lot grandfathering), must allow "no holding period" (Austria since 2022-03-01), and every country page needs a "Stand" line and a changelog, as Blockpit does. The marketing hook "tax-free after one year" cannot be hard-coded for Germany.

3. **"Free core forever" is not free of data cost.** CoinGecko's Demo is described as "testing and exploration", is capped at 10,000 calls a month and 365 days of history, and carries no commercial licence; a product with a paid tier needs at least Basic ($35/month). Budget $35 to $129 a month for data before revenue, or accept the Demo risk only for the pre-revenue phase. Attribution ("Price data by CoinGecko" or equivalent, 10 pt or larger) must sit next to every price and chart, including on the no-account pages.

4. **"Plan plus tax-holding awareness" is not entirely unoccupied.** 21bitcoin ships a savings plan with a Haltefrist-Tracker (BTC only, DE/AT, custodial, sees only its own trades) and Blockpit Plus ships a holding-period tracker plus a sale simulator (DE only, backward-looking, no plan). The differentiated combination is narrower than the brief implies: multi-coin, multi-venue, custody-free, forward-looking, country-aware, no account required.

5. **EUR, GBP and CHF prices from CoinGecko are converted, not traded.** CoinGecko quotes non-USD currencies by conversion; Kraken and Bitstamp EUR pairs are traded prices. An "honesty" product must state which it shows and when the daily close is taken (00:00 UTC for CoinGecko, exchange-local for candles) and which FX rate converts history (ECB reference rate, working days only).

6. **CSV import as a paid-tier feature cuts against table stakes.** Every tracker in section 3 offers CSV import in its free tier, and 21bitcoin, Trade Republic and most brokers export CSV only. Keeping import paid is defensible for sync and reminders but will cost onboarding of exactly the disciplined saver the product targets; consider a free single-file import and charging for repeated or multi-venue imports.

7. **Portugal is smaller than the ECB headline suggests.** ECB surveys put Portuguese ownership at 12% (SPACE 2024) or up to 21% (CES household measure, secondary reading), but the face-to-face national financial-literacy survey (CNSF/Banco de Portugal 2023) finds 1.5% holding crypto. Treat the PT market as early and small, and do not quote the 21% figure without its caveat. The Portuguese 365-day rule also has an unresolved detail that the sell-window logic must not hard-code: sources disagree on whether crypto-to-crypto swaps reset the clock; confirm with a PT tax adviser before encoding.

8. **The European DCA habit is unmeasured.** No official statistic asks about recurring crypto buying; the only direct measure is PwC Strategy& 2026 (50% of active crypto investors in five markets use savings plans; 37% of German crypto investors), based on existing investors. "Most European savers already DCA" cannot be claimed; "half of active crypto investors use savings plans" can, with the caveat.

9. **Binance is not an EU fallback.** Its public data is CC BY-NC-SA with commercial use "strictly excluded", and its EU service was impaired by the MiCA outcome in mid-2026; drop it from any architecture diagram.
